CIAM Patterns & Adaptive Authentication

Customer IAM is not workforce IAM. The users are millions of consumers who didn't read a manual, churn if login hurts, and have privacy rights. These are the patterns that define a CIAM build — registration, social, passwordless, risk-based auth, and consent — and how Ping implements each.

👥 What is CIAM vs workforce IAM — and why do consumer patterns need a different architecture?

CIAM and workforce IAM solve different problems: workforce manages thousands of employees who tolerate friction; CIAM manages millions of consumers who abandon a slow login, demand privacy (GDPR/CCPA consent), and turn friction into lost revenue. So the architecture prioritizes scale, variety and adaptability. Key Ping patterns: progressive registration (ask the minimum, request more at moments of value), social login / identity brokering (AM runs the OIDC flow to Google/Apple and links a managed user — one-click signup), passwordless (magic links, passkeys/WebAuthn, push MFA as journey nodes), risk-based adaptive auth (a risk score branches the journey: low = password, high = step-up), and consent management (a Consent node captures a timestamped record IDM stores; APIs check it before processing PII).

  • CIAM ≠ workforce IAM — millions of consumers; friction = lost revenue + privacy law.
  • Progressive registration + social login — minimal signup; AM brokers Google/Apple.
  • Passwordless + risk-based auth — passkeys/magic links; risk score branches the journey.
  • Consent management — timestamped records in IDM; APIs check before using PII.

In CIAM / Your Job: In your Angular + Spring + Ping stack: social login means Angular has no Google SDK — AM brokers it and Angular just gets a standard Ping JWT. Risk-based auth puts a risk-score claim in the JWT — gate high-risk operations (large transfers, account changes) on it. Consent means your API checks IDM's consent records before sending marketing or processing PII (a GDPR/CCPA violation is a legal event, not a bug). Progressive registration means your profile page updates the IDM record incrementally.

2. Registration & progressive profiling

Ask for as little as possible

Every extra field at sign-up drops conversion. Collect the minimum (email + password, or just a social button), create the account, get them in.

Enrich over time (progressively)

Need a phone number for SMS later? Ask then, once, inside a journey's Progressive Profile node — not at the gate. The profile fills in as trust & need grow.

3. Social login & account linking

"Continue with Google/Apple/Facebook" — the user authenticates at the social IdP; Ping receives the federated identity and either creates a local profile or links it to an existing one (matched by verified email). In a journey this is the Social Provider Handler + Identify Existing User nodes. The win: no new password, instant trust signal, higher conversion. The trap: account linking must verify ownership, or you've created a takeover path.

4. Passwordless & MFA

🔑 Passkeys (WebAuthn)

Phishing-resistant, device-bound biometrics. The modern default — login with FaceID/fingerprint, no password at all.

📲 Push / OTP

PingOne MFA push approval, or a TOTP/SMS/email one-time code. Common step-up factors triggered by risk.

✉️ Magic links

Email a one-time login link — passwordless for low-risk consumer flows; weak as a sole high-assurance factor.

In a journey these are just nodes; which one fires is decided by risk (next section) and the required auth level.

5. Interactive: risk-based (adaptive) authentication

Toggle the signals PingOne Protect (the risk engine) would observe at login. The score updates live and a journey's risk node routes to allow, step-up, or deny.

This is the heart of CIAM security: most logins sail through with zero friction; MFA appears only when the signals justify it. Step-up beats "MFA for everyone."

6. Consent & preference management

CIAM owns the legal surface: consent (did the user agree to this processing?), consent receipts (proof, with timestamp & version), marketing preferences, and data-subject rights (export/delete). Ping models these as managed attributes + consent objects the user controls in self-service. Get it wrong and it's a fine, not just a bug — so consent state is first-class profile data, not an afterthought.

7. Recap

👥 CIAM = identity for millions of consumers: self-service registration that asks for the minimum and progressively profiles; social login + safe account linking; passwordless/passkeys and MFA as journey nodes; risk-based auth (PingOne Protect) that adds friction only when signals warrant a step-up; and first-class consent & preferences for privacy law. Frictionless by default, secure on demand.
